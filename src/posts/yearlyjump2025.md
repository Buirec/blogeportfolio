**Overview**
</br></br>
Little late this year, but honestly one of the years I wasn't as in tune with new Jump series as usual, with the ending of massive series like My Hero Academia and Jujutsu Kaisen, and relatively popular longrunners like Undead Unluck and Mission Yozakura Family, the magazine was in full crisis mode trying to find what franchise it can prop up with an equivalent future ahead of it and a lot of the time you just get a lot of series that don't really have a lot of shelf life or staying power as the editors throw a bunch of new serializations at the wall to see what sticks. While we can never be sure what series that's currently in Jump that will secure the same space without an anime and a million movies to really rake in merchandise, we do have a few serious contenders going by strong volume sales and high potential for multimedia franchise hype; namely Kagurabachi and Ichi the Witch. While I still don't like Kagurabachi, it looks to be the textbook WSJ Cinderella story, a new author with a massive hit battle shonen that tickles the hype and aura part of it's audiences brain, and there's always a place for a series like that. The other serious contender is Ichi the Witch, which manages to impress me every week with strong art and panel composition, great characterisation, and imaginative and interesting battles. </br></br>
In terms of trends, well, this is much harder to place, looking at the series that started this year, there really isn't a defining trait that other years had like the orientalist themed supernatural battle manga that populated the last few years. Last year I asserted that romcoms were coming back in vogue, but most of those series didn't survive and none have propped up in 2025 so maybe that was jumping the gun a bit. I'm more surprised that Jump seems to be struggling now to find a consistent identity, while normally I praise a wide variety the series that have propped up feel less like strategic experimentation and more like filling up slots. That's not to say there weren't series I didn't like that started this year. The highlight for me is Someone Hertz, a cosy one liner comedy manga centered around two radio comedy enthusiasts bonding through their shared interest while trying to develop their own skill at jokes, there's a strong throughline in the development between the two leads, as well as a goal that drives the forward momentum of the manga, despite the warm and sleepy atmosphere, which is a strong showing of WSJ fundamentals with a less conventional series. Other series I think have potential are Hero Girl, and Harukaze Mound, although I suspect the latter is due to the drought of good sports manga being localised right now. That being said, in terms of numbers and buzz, the most successful series starting this year is... Jujutsu Kaisen again...
</br></br>
But what about Jump Plus? My golden boy? I'll admit I've especially found it hard to keep up with new series this year in J+, basically everything I tried I didn't really stick past the first few chapters. I will admit, there was a lot of variety as usual, but there wasn't anything I view as particularly exceptional, particular because of the series that are still running that manage to top themselves again this year. Make the Exorcist Fall in Love is just the best running manga right now, and hits a back to back of incredible arcs with incredible payoffs for their characters, I cannot praise enough how good this manga is, and what a crime it is that not many people are reading it. Other series that I think are worth a read and had great runs this year include Monochrome Days, Hero Organization, No Gyaru in This Class, Centuria, and of course the runner up GOAT Girl Meets Rock.
